\section{What the method does and where it lives}
\label{sec:what}

A photon inside an optically thick cell scatters many times before it leaves, and the
in-cell random walk of \texttt{mrw\_guide.tex} replaces those scatterings only when the
cell is many mean free paths thick, paying a dead zone at every face.  Discrete
diffusion Monte Carlo (DDMC; Densmore et al.\ 2007) replaces the photon in a thick cell
by a \emph{walker}: it has a cell, an energy, a weight and a time, and no
position or direction.  It waits in the cell for an exponential time whose rate is the
sum of the leakage rates through the six faces, then hops to the neighbour the chosen
face leads to; if that neighbour is thin, it emerges there as an ordinary transport
photon on the face.  A transport photon entering a thick cell is converted the other
way with the probability of the asymptotic diffusion limit.  The lattice reproduces the
diffusion equation on the grid to first order in the cell size, cell by cell, with each
cell's own properties, and costs one draw per cell traversed rather than one per
scattering.

For Compton scattering the Thomson extinction is grey, so the leakage rates do not
depend on the walker's energy and the energy decouples from the walk: after a dwell
of comoving path $ct$ the energy is drawn from the Green's function of the Kompaneets
equation at $y=\theta\,n_e\sigma_T\,ct$, exactly as the random-walk step does, with the
free-free survival from the same table (\texttt{kgreens.cpp};
\texttt{doc/monte\_carlo/kompaneets/}).  No energy groups exist.

The code is \texttt{src/monte\_carlo/ddmc.cpp} with \texttt{ddmc.hpp}, off unless
\key{<ddmc> enable} is set, and reaches the rest of the module through the hooks of
the plan's Section~2.9 and no others: a branch at the top of each pusher's per-photon
loop that runs the walk while the walker's flag is set, a conversion test where a
transport photon's \texttt{UpdateZone} enters a new cell, the integer photon column
\texttt{ddmcp} registered only when enabled, one guard in
\texttt{TransferPhotonsOnBlock}, one call in \texttt{SetupBlockFromFluid} that builds
the per-cell rate table, and the keys and report lines in \texttt{montecarlo.cpp}.
With the feature off, lists, moments and payloads are byte-identical to a build before
it existed.

\section{Conventions and units}
\label{sec:units}

The extinction $\chi$ is the comoving Thomson extinction in cm$^{-1}$, $\chi=n_e
\sigma_T$ with $n_e$ the electron density in \texttt{species(0,k,j,i)}, as the random
walk uses it; the free-free absorption enters only through the Green's function's sink
(Section~\ref{sec:energy}).  Lengths in the rate table are physical: cell widths in
code length times \key{l\_cgs}; under the general pusher the widths are the proper
lengths of the cell's coordinate edges in the cell-centre tetrad of the normal observer,
which \texttt{GeneralPusher::MRWFrame} builds (\texttt{fr.W}), and in the
spherical-polar pusher the box $\dd r\times r\,\dd\theta\times r\sin\theta\,\dd\phi$
that \texttt{CellGeometry} makes of the cell.  The diffusion coefficient is
$D=1/(3\chi)$, a length.  Times are comoving proper times inside the lattice; the dwell
is converted to coordinate time once per hop (Section~\ref{sec:gr}).  Rates are kept per
unit comoving path $c\,t$, in cm$^{-1}$, so that a leakage ``opacity'' $k_f$ competes
with the extinction on the same footing and the dwell is drawn as a path, $c\,t = -\ln
\xi/k_{\rm tot}$, with $\xi$ uniform.

\section{The lattice and its rates}
\label{sec:rates}

Let $E_c$ be the radiation energy density of cell $c$ in its comoving frame.  The
diffusion equation on the grid, in the finite-volume form of Densmore et al.\ (2007)
and Smith et al.\ (2018, their equation 10), is
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1}{c}\frac{\partial E_c}{\partial t}
  = \sum_{f} k_f^{(n\to c)}\,\frac{V_n}{V_c}\,E_n - \sum_f k_f^{(c\to n)}\,E_c
  - \kappa_{a,c}E_c ,
\end{equation}
where the sums run over the faces $f$ of the cell, $n$ is the neighbour across $f$, and
the leakage rate per unit path from $c$ into $n$ across a face normal to axis $i$ is
\begin{equation}
  k_f^{(c\to n)} = \frac{2D_c/\Delta_{c,i}}{\Delta_{c,i} + \Delta_{n,i}\,D_c/D_n}
  = \frac{2}{3\,\Delta_{c,i}\,(\chi_c\Delta_{c,i} + \chi_n\Delta_{n,i})} .
  \label{eq:kint}
\end{equation}
This is Fick's law through the two half-cells, $F = -(E_n - E_c)/\bigl(\Delta_c/2D_c +
\Delta_n/2D_n\bigr)$, with the part proportional to $E_c$ divided by the cell width.
In a uniform medium it is $1/(3\chi\Delta^2)$, and a walker makes about
$(\chi\Delta)^2/3$ Thomson paths' worth of dwell per hop, which is the number of
scatterings a hop replaces.

A face whose far side is not a diffusion cell (a transport cell, a face to another
refinement level under the rule of Section~\ref{sec:faces}, or a mesh boundary) is a
\emph{boundary face}.  Its rate comes from the asymptotic diffusion-limit boundary
condition of Habetler \& Matkowsky (1975), which Densmore et al.\ (2007) and
Abdikamalov et al.\ (2012, their equations 120 to 127) use in the same way: at the face
the mean intensity obeys
\begin{equation}
  \int_0^1 W(\mu)\,I_b(\mu)\,\dd\mu = J_{\rm face} - \frac{\lambda}{\chi}
  \left.\frac{\partial J}{\partial n}\right|_{\rm face},\qquad
  W(\mu)\simeq \mu + \tfrac32\mu^2,\qquad \lambda = 0.7104,
  \label{eq:abc}
\end{equation}
with $I_b$ the intensity incident from outside, $n$ the outward normal and
$\int_0^1 W\,\dd\mu = 1$.  Replacing the derivative by $(J_{\rm face} - J_c)/(\Delta_c/2)$
and the face flux by Fick's law over the half-cell, $H = -(2/3\chi_c\Delta_c)(J_{\rm face}
- J_c)$, gives
\begin{equation}
  H = \frac{2}{3\chi_c\Delta_c + 6\lambda}\Bigl(J_c - \int_0^1 W(\mu)\,I_b(\mu)\,\dd\mu\Bigr),
\end{equation}
so the leakage rate out through a boundary face is
\begin{equation}
  k_f^{\rm (bdy)} = \frac{2}{3\,\Delta_{c,i}\,(\chi_c\Delta_{c,i} + 2\lambda)}
  = \frac{2D_c/\Delta_{c,i}}{\Delta_{c,i} + 2\lambda/\chi_c} ,
  \label{eq:kbdy}
\end{equation}
the interior form with the far half-cell replaced by the extrapolation distance
$\lambda/\chi_c = 2.13\,D_c$, and the incident intensity enters as a source with
weight $W(\mu)$, which is the conversion probability of Section~\ref{sec:interface}.
(The plan of 2026-10-06 had $2\lambda D_c$ in the denominator, a factor three short.)

A cell is a diffusion cell when
\begin{equation}
  \tau_{\rm cell} = \chi_c \min_i \Delta_{c,i} \ge \tau_{\rm ddmc}
\end{equation}
with \key{<ddmc> tau} the threshold; the thinnest dimension governs because leakage
is fastest across it.  Densmore et al.\ validate the interface down to $\tau_{\rm cell}$
of about 2 and Smith et al.\ suggest 5; D1 scans 2, 3, 5 and 10 against the analog run
and sets the default.  One threshold serves both directions; the asymmetry between
entering and leaving is carried by the probabilities, and a second key is added only if
D1 asks for it.

The rate table is built once per transport cycle in \texttt{SetupBlockFromFluid},
after the number densities, as \texttt{ddmc\_rate(7,k,j,i)}: six face rates and
their sum, over the active cells, with the neighbour's $\chi_n$ and $\Delta_n$ read from
the ghost zones.  Ghost zones are valid for this: \texttt{Mesh::Initialize}
(\texttt{mesh.cpp}, the block from the conserved-variable exchange through
\texttt{ProlongateBoundaries} to \texttt{ConservedToPrimitive} over the extended
bounds and \texttt{ApplyPhysicalBoundaries}) fills the primitives in the ghost zones
from the neighbours, across levels, before \texttt{MonteCarlo::Initialize} runs
(\texttt{main.cpp}), and the former skip of the inversion for GR Monte Carlo builds is
gone; \texttt{FillBounds} then spans the ghosts when \texttt{rho}, \texttt{tgas} and
\texttt{species} are filled.  A problem generator's \texttt{ExcludeCells} must zero
the species over the same bounds, which D1 asserts on a two-block run by comparing a
ghost $\chi$ with the neighbour's active value.  Under the general pusher the widths
need a tetrad per cell, including the ghost layer; the per-cell transformations of
\texttt{ComputeTransformations} are where that goes.  The table is rebuilt on restart
and redistribution like the opacity tables, so nothing is carried.

\section{The walker and its life}
\label{sec:walker}

A walker is a \texttt{Photon} whose integer column \texttt{ddmcp} is 1.
Its position is the centre of its cell, which keeps every consumer of a position
working unchanged: \texttt{UpdateZone}, the boundary functions, the block exchange, the
outputs, the load balancer's payload and the restart record.  Its direction columns
are unused and its coherency tensor and Stokes parameters are zero.  Its energy
\texttt{ep} is the comoving energy in its cell's frame; its weight, time
\texttt{x0p}, budget \texttt{dtp}, scattering count \texttt{nscp} and type are what
they are for a photon.  Its flight columns (\texttt{taup}, \texttt{strp}) are reset
at emergence.

The walk, in the pusher's per-photon loop while \texttt{ddmcp} is set:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Read the cell's rates.  Form the path budget $c\,t_b$: the cycle budget
  \texttt{dtp} converted to comoving path (Section~\ref{sec:gr}), and the Kompaneets
  table's $y$ range, $c\,t \le y_{\max}/(\theta n_e\sigma_T)$.  (The random walk's
  \key{accel\_pmax} has no analogue; the lattice has no domain survival.)
\item Draw the path $c\,t = -\ln\xi/k_{\rm tot}$.  If it exceeds $c\,t_b$, set $c\,t =
  c\,t_b$ and stay in the cell (the walker keeps its flag; a budget stop returns it
  to the block loop, a table cap continues the walk).
\item Update the energy and weight over the dwell (Section~\ref{sec:energy}); deposit
  the moments and source terms (Section~\ref{sec:moments}); advance \texttt{x0p} and
  \texttt{dtp}; add $n_e\sigma_T\,ct$ to \texttt{nscp} and to the report's count of
  scatterings replaced.
\item If a budget stop or absorption ended the dwell, return.  Otherwise choose the
  face with probability $k_f/k_{\rm tot}$ and hop:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item interior face: translate the walker by one cell (its comoving energy
    unchanged, Section~\ref{sec:moving}), deposit the face
    counters (Section~\ref{sec:moments}); if the neighbour is a ghost cell, the walker
    is now outside the block and \texttt{UpdateZone} buffers it to the neighbour
    block, where it lands in the face cell with its flag and continues;
  \item boundary face: emergence (Section~\ref{sec:interface}), which clears the flag;
    the loop then continues as a transport photon with a fresh optical depth, and a
    mesh boundary is met by the ordinary boundary function on the first step.
  \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}
The general pusher invalidates its cached metric and connection after a hop, as the
random-walk branch does.  \texttt{UserWorkInMove} is called after each hop, so a
generator's hook (the horizon test of \texttt{mc\_readhdf\_gr}) sees the walker at
its new centre.  \texttt{TransferPhotonsOnBlock} skips absorption and scattering for a
walker that returns from \texttt{Move} with the flag set.

\section{Faces that are not interior}
\label{sec:faces}

\textbf{Mesh boundaries.}  A face on the mesh boundary is a boundary face whatever the
boundary condition: the walker emerges through it and the boundary function
(escape, absorb, periodic, polar) acts on the transport photon as it does today.  A
periodic neighbour is in fact a real cell whose ghost values are filled, so periodic
faces could be interior; they are left as boundary faces in the first build for
simplicity and the report counts them.

\textbf{Refinement levels.}  A face whose neighbour block is at another level (known
from \texttt{pbval->nblevel} for the block's outer layer of cells; interior faces of a
block never jump levels) is a boundary face in both directions: a walker reaching it
emerges through it, a transport photon crossing it into a thick cell converts by the
usual probability.  This is exact and loses efficiency only at level jumps; the report
counts hops stopped there, and the split-face rates of Densmore et al.\ (2007), which
weight the four fine sub-faces by area, are the follow-up if the count is large on the
XRB, whose snapshot has eight levels.

\textbf{Block faces at the same level} are interior faces: the rate uses the ghost
neighbour, and the hop is the BUFFERED exchange.

\section{Energy and absorption over a dwell}
\label{sec:energy}

With $\theta = kT_c/m_ec^2$ of the cell and the walker's comoving energy $x_i = E/kT_c$,
a dwell of path $ct$ has Compton parameter $y = \theta\,n_e\sigma_T\,ct$ and the new
energy is $x_f kT_c$ with $x_f$ from \texttt{kgreens->Quantile(xi, y, lam, u)}; the
weight is multiplied by \texttt{kgreens->Survival(xi, y, lam)} when the absorption is
free-free (\texttt{lam} the cell's free-free parameter as \texttt{MRWStep} forms it from
\texttt{ff\_cell}), and by $\exp(-\kappa_a\,ct)$ for a grey absorption that is not
free-free.  Under \key{abs\_method = prob} the survival is a probability instead and the
walker is ABSORBED with its complement; under \key{weight} it is a weight and the
roulette of \texttt{RouletteOrAbsorb} applies below \key{minweight}.  The guards are
those of \texttt{MRWTrigger} and \texttt{MRWStep}: $\theta\le0.03$, $x\theta\le0.05$,
$x$ inside the table, the sink strength within the table's range, $n_e>0$; a walker
whose cell fails them is not converted (a transport photon in such a cell stays a
transport photon, and the conversion test includes the guards), and the report counts
the declines by reason.  A long dwell is capped at the table's $y_{\max}$ and the walk
continues with a second draw, so no energy is ever extrapolated.

Only the Thomson-grey regime is covered; a Klein-Nishina dependence of the leakage
rates on energy would be a per-group rate table, which the XRB bands below 10 keV do
not need.

\section{Moments and source terms}
\label{sec:moments}

Required: energy densities, fluxes and the radiation force must come out of diffusion
cells.

\textbf{Energy density}, per dwell, by the time-spent estimator:
\texttt{UpdateMomentsMRW(pphot, ct, $\bar E$, beta\_tet, gam, ip)} with $\bar E$ the
mean of the energies before and after the dwell, which deposits the isotropic comoving
field $E_c = w\bar E\,ct/c$ boosted to the lab (its Section~4 formulas).

\textbf{Flux}, by the face-hop estimator.  Each hop through a face normal to axis $i$
adds $s\,wE$ to that face's counter of each of the two cells sharing it, $s=\pm1$ the
direction of crossing along $+i$ and $E$ the energy carried across (the leaving cell's
comoving energy; the frame change of the hop is second order in the flux).  Over an
integration time the counter divided by the face area and \texttt{tint} is the flux
through the face, and the cell-centred comoving flux along $i$ is the mean of the
cell's two faces.  It is the discrete Fick flux of the lattice, $F=-cD\nabla E$ on the
grid, so it is consistent with what the lattice solves and needs no model.  The
counters are a per-block array \texttt{ddmc\_flux(6,k,j,i)} in the comoving frame;
both sides of a block face record every crossing themselves (the leaving block when the
walker leaves, the receiving block when it arrives), so no communication is needed.
At normalization the cell's comoving $(E_c, F_c, P_c = E_c\delta/3)$ is boosted into
the lab moments with the cell's velocity matrices of \texttt{ComovingFrameMatrix}, the
isotropic part having been deposited per dwell already, so only the flux term is
added there.

\textbf{Pressure}: $E_c/3$ on the diagonal in the comoving frame, the diffusion
closure, which is the assumption of a diffusion cell and not a measurement.

\textbf{Radiation force.}  The momentum deposited in the gas of a diffusion cell per
unit volume and time is $\chi_c F_c/c$ with $\chi_c$ the total extinction, equal to
$-\nabla E_c/3$ by Fick's law.  With
the flux from the face counters, each hop deposits
\begin{equation}
  \delta p_i = s\,\frac{wE\,\chi_c\,\Delta_{c,i}}{2c}
\end{equation}
into \texttt{sourceterms(MCRS}$_i$\texttt{)} of each cell sharing the face, each with
its own $\chi$ and width (the factor $1/2$ is the face average), and the same into
\texttt{MCRF}$_i$, since the flux force and the momentum force coincide on the
lattice.  The sign convention is that of \texttt{UpdateSourceTerms}, where the gas
gains minus the photon's momentum change.  A photon converting at the start of a flight
is placed at the cell's centre, and \texttt{DDMCConvert} deposits that move in the flux
(the move as the sign times the half-width) so that the flux remains the sum of every
move of the sample's energy, but not in the force: the photon reached its scattering
point by streaming and the force of that leg is in its flight's deposit
(Section~\ref{sec:r4}, the force measured).  \textbf{Energy exchange}
\texttt{MCRS0}: $w_{\rm after}E_{\rm after} - w_{\rm before}E_{\rm before}$ per dwell,
through \texttt{UpdateSourceTerms} with the energy and weight before and after, which
also covers the absorbed energy.  The per-walker contributions that
\texttt{sourceterms\_error} holds back until the walker leaves a cell accumulate over
its hops in that cell unchanged.

\textbf{Known limits.}  The flux in a thick cell is the difference of two large hop
counts, with a statistical error set by the gross hop rate; the analog flux there is the
residual of near-isotropic flights and is no better.  The scattering-binned spectral
moments (\texttt{Jnu}, \texttt{mom\_flag\_scat}) see only the energies before and
after a dwell.  Fick's law from the energy-density estimator of neighbouring cells is a
cross-check in the tests, not the product, since it fails in interface cells where
transport flights also contribute.

\section{The interface}
\label{sec:interface}

\textbf{Conversion}, when a transport photon reaches a face of its cell beyond which
lies a diffusion cell $n$, with direction cosine $\mu$ to the face normal.  From
equation~(\ref{eq:abc}) the incident intensity enters the diffusion solution with weight
$W(\mu)$, and the part of the face flux it drives is $4\pi\,(2/(3\chi_n\Delta_{n,i} +
6\lambda))\,\langle W I_b\rangle$, where $\langle W I_b\rangle = \int_0^1 W I_b\,\dd\mu$
is an average normalized like the mean intensity, over the full sphere, while the
energy a beam carries through the face is $\mu I_b\,\dd\Omega$ with the hemisphere's
$2\pi$.  The ratio is the conversion probability,
\begin{equation}
  P(\mu) = \frac{4}{3\chi_n\Delta_{n,i} + 6\lambda}\Bigl(1 + \tfrac32\mu\Bigr)
  = \frac{4}{3\,(\chi_n\Delta_{n,i} + 2\lambda)}\Bigl(1 + \tfrac32\mu\Bigr),
  \label{eq:pconv}
\end{equation}
Smith et al.'s equation 18, $2(2/3+\mu)/(\chi\Delta + 2\lambda)$, and the form of
Densmore et al.\ (2007); it derives from the same finite difference as the boundary
leakage rate~(\ref{eq:kbdy}), and the two must be used together.  The check that
fixes the factor: a diffusion cell in equilibrium with an isotropic field $I_0$
outside leaks $4\pi I_0\cdot 2/(3(\chi\Delta+2\lambda))$ per unit area and receives
$2\pi I_0\int_0^1 \mu P(\mu)\,\dd\mu = 8\pi I_0/(3(\chi\Delta + 2\lambda))$, equal
only with the 4.  (The draft of this note had a 2, from counting the hemisphere
twice; built that way, the lattice lost energy at every interface and the Thomson
sphere escaped 5 to 13 percent too fast, while a cube with no interface reproduced
its exact lattice solution.  The factor was found on 2026-10-28 by that pair of
tests, the sphere's error vanishing once it was corrected.)  $P$ exceeds 1 below
$\chi\Delta \simeq 1.9$ at normal incidence, which is why the threshold must stay at
2 or above; a value above 1 is clamped and counted.  A converted photon becomes a
walker at the cell's centre with its energy transformed into the cell's frame.  A
photon not converted is returned into the cell it came from with an isotropic inward
direction, at the face, keeping its flight state.

\textbf{Emergence}, when a walker hops through a boundary face.  The photon is placed
at a uniform point on the face, on the transport side, with the flag cleared, its
energy taken to the lab with the drawn direction (Section~\ref{sec:moving}), weight
carried, Stokes parameters
zero, \texttt{taup} $=-1$ and \texttt{strp} $=0$ so the pusher draws a fresh flight.
Its outward direction is drawn from one of two distributions, \key{<ddmc> emergence}:
\key{asymptotic}, $W(\mu) = \mu + \tfrac32\mu^2$ on the outward hemisphere, the
flux-weighted emergent intensity $I\propto\tfrac23+\mu$ of the diffusion limit and the
distribution consistent with equation~(\ref{eq:abc}); or \key{isotropic}, uniform in
$\mu\in(0,1)$, which is what Densmore et al.\ (2007), Abdikamalov et al.\ (2012) and
Smith et al.\ (2018) use.  The first is the expected default; the disk atmosphere
against Feautrier decides in D1, since the emergent intensity versus $\mu$ is the one
observable that sees this choice.  $W(\mu)$ is sampled by solving
$\mu^2/2 + \mu^3/2 = \xi$ for $\mu$, a few Newton steps from $\mu=\xi^{1/2}$.

\section{A moving medium}
\label{sec:moving}

The lattice is in the comoving frame of each cell: rates, dwell, energy and moments.
A walker's energy is the comoving energy in the frame of the fluid where it is, and
three things happen to it that do not happen in a medium at rest.

\textbf{The walker is carried by its fluid.}  Over a dwell of comoving path $ct$ the
walker's lab position moves by $\gamma\beta\,ct$, with $\gamma\beta$ the fluid's value
interpolated linearly across the cell along each axis between the two neighbours (so
that at a face it is the mean of the two cells' values, which is what the one-way
fluxes of the analog transport across that face add up to).  The dwell is cut at the
comoving path at which the drift reaches a face of the cell, and the walker then
crosses that face (\texttt{DDMCAdvect}): into a diffusion cell as a walker, placed a
hair inside the neighbour (at the neighbour's centre along the axis when that cell's
flow opposes the crossing, so a converging flow does not shuttle it across the face);
through any other face as a transport photon, isotropic outward in the comoving frame
from where it is on the face.  An advective crossing deposits no flux and no force: the
lab moments of the dwell already carry the advective flux $\tfrac{4}{3}\gamma^2 v E_c$
through the boost of the isotropic comoving field.  A diffusive hop keeps the walker's
offset from the centre along the face's axis (a translation by one cell), so that hops
neither advance nor set back the drift; the walker is at the centre only where it
entered the lattice as a photon.  This is the advection $\nabla\cdot(n\boldsymbol{v})$
of the walker number, exact in expectation, with no numerical diffusion.

\textbf{Energy over a dwell.}  Isotropic radiation in a fluid with velocity divergence
loses $\tfrac{1}{3}\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v}$ of its energy per unit time to the work
on the fluid, so over a dwell $E \to E\exp(-ct\,\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\beta}/3)$ with
the cell's divergence from centred differences of the neighbours' velocities (stored
with the rates, \texttt{DDMC\_RATE\_DIV}); the change goes to the energy source term
like the Compton one.  This is the whole first-order energy effect of the flow on a
diffusing walker: the redshift a photon accrues is the time integral of
$\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{n}\!:\!\nabla\boldsymbol{\beta}$ along its path, which for the
isotropic wandering of a dwell is the divergence term and nothing else.

\textbf{No frame change at a hop.}  A walker that hops or is carried into a cell of
another velocity keeps its energy.  The crossing photons of the analog transport are
relabelled into the new cell's frame by $-\Delta\boldsymbol{\beta}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}$,
but relative to the fluid at the face, which moves at the mean of the two cells, that
relabelling is a bookkeeping offset of $\pm\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta\boldsymbol{\beta}\cdot
\boldsymbol{n}$ that the wandering to the next centre undoes; the physical loss is the
time integral above.  A hop transform through a direction drawn from $W(\mu)$ (the
first D4 build, and the mean-cosine formula of Smith et al.\ 2018, their equations 19
and 20) adds a spurious redshift of $1.42/\tau_{\rm cell}$ times the adiabatic one, and
on its own, without the divergence term, gives only that fraction of the cooling.
Conversion (a photon with a direction) and emergence (a direction drawn) do change
frame, through \texttt{TransformToComoving} and \texttt{TransformToCoordinate} as
before.

The dwell's lab time is $\gamma$ times the comoving time (Section~\ref{sec:gr}).  What
is still not represented: the Lorentz contraction of the cell along the flow in the
comoving rates (second order), and the velocity gradient within a cell beyond the
linear interpolation of the drift.  What the moving sphere measured, and the two
earlier attempts the measurements rejected, are in Section~\ref{sec:d4}.

\section{Curved spacetime}
\label{sec:gr}

Under the general pusher the cell's widths are the proper lengths of its coordinate
edges in the normal observer's tetrad at the cell centre (\texttt{fr.W} of
\texttt{GeneralPusher::MRWFrame}), $\theta$ and $n_e$ are the cell's comoving values,
and the dwell is a comoving proper time $t$.  Coordinate time advances by
$\Delta x^0 = \gamma\,ct/(c\,\alpha)$ in code units with $\gamma = u^{\hat t}$ the
fluid's Lorentz factor on the tetrad and $\alpha$ the lapse, exactly as
\texttt{MRWStep} does (\texttt{dl = gam*ct/l\_cgs/fr.lapse}); \texttt{dtp} decreases by
the same.  The drift, the divergence term and the frame of the interface test of
Section~\ref{sec:moving} are written for flat space and are D5's to carry onto the
tetrads, where the gravitational shift joins the Doppler one.  Inside the horizon of
\texttt{mc\_readhdf\_gr} the species are zero and no cell is a diffusion cell.

\section{Time and dynamic runs}
\label{sec:time}

The waiting times are physical.  In a static run \texttt{tmax} is huge and no budget
binds.  In a dynamic run the budget is \texttt{dtp}, and a walker whose dwell would
exceed it stops in its cell with the flag set and returns to the block loop.

Finding of D0, by reading the three pushers, \texttt{TransferPhotonsOnBlock} and
\texttt{FinishRound}: today \texttt{dtp} is only the exit condition of the pusher's
step loop.  A transport photon that exhausts it returns EVOLVING, is scattered in place
by the block loop at every pass, and keeps the round from finishing, since the round
ends only when no photon is on any list.  So the budget is not a stop today, and
dynamic runs work because \texttt{tmax} (the hydro step) does not bind on their
photons.  A true stop, for photons and walkers alike, needs a held status that the
round accounting treats as done and the next cycle resumes, which is outside the
plan's hook list and is a decision for the user before D6.  Until then the walker's
budget stop is implemented as the photon's is (the walker sits, flagged, and is
skipped by the block loop), and the Compton phases run static.

\section{What D1 built, where it departs from the sections above}
\label{sec:d1}

Phase D1 (2026-10-28, branch \texttt{ddmc}): grey elastic scattering in flat space in
the Cartesian and spherical-polar pushers, one file \texttt{ddmc.cpp} plus the hooks of
Section~\ref{sec:what}.  The departures from the design, each for a reason found in
building or testing:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Conversion at the start of a flight.}  A transport photon beginning a
  fresh flight after a scattering in a diffusion cell is converted with probability 1
  at the cell's centre.  A photon emitted in a diffusion cell flies its first flight
  before it can be converted, so a direct escape from the top layer of the lattice
  keeps the position it was born at; the walker's centre placement is the one
  $O(\Delta)$ error the method has for emergent light from an emitting medium, and it
  falls with the threshold as the light from the first lattice layer does.  Emission
  inside diffusion cells therefore makes walkers already, which the plan had in D6;
  a photon that reached a diffusion cell through an edge or corner crossing, or across
  a block face from a neighbour that could not test it, is caught the same way at its
  next scattering.
\item \textbf{The face test precedes the crossing.}  \texttt{DDMCFaceTest} runs on the
  target cell before \texttt{MovePhotonToNextZone}, so a ghost cell across a same-level
  block face is tested too and a conversion into it is buffered to that block.  A
  returned photon and an emerged photon are placed $10^{-7}$ of the cell's width inside
  the cell (the reflecting boundaries' device), so the crossing is the pusher's own
  first step and every boundary function acts as usual.
\item \textbf{The flag carries the arrival.}  \texttt{ddmcp} is 1 for a resident
  walker and $2 + f$ for one that has just hopped (or converted) through a block face
  and enters its new cell through face $f$: the receiving block then makes the
  entering deposit and the frame change, so both sides of a block face record every
  crossing without communication.  The lab energy and direction ride along in the
  unused columns while the walker is in transit.
\item \textbf{The mask over the ghosts.}  \texttt{ddmc\_cell} covers the ghost zone,
  real only across a same-level block face (\texttt{block\_bcs} and \texttt{nblevel});
  the problem generator's \texttt{accel\_mask} is shared with the random walk and
  \texttt{mc\_sphere\_isoth} now fills it over the ghosts, so two blocks agree on a
  cell.  A walker whose cell is no longer a diffusion cell (a rebuilt lattice, or a
  block that disagrees with its neighbour's ghost) is released as an isotropic transport
  photon at the centre, counted.
\item \textbf{Moments.}  The hop flux goes straight into the lab moments (and the
  comoving ones when they are accumulated), with a first-order boost when the cell
  moves, rather than through a separate face array; the energy density of a dwell
  uses the mean weight along it under absorption, $w(1-e^{-\kappa_a ct})/(\kappa_a
  ct)$; the scatterings a dwell stands for are counted up to the absorption in
  expectation.  The pending force-error contribution is flushed at each change of
  cell, so a walker's visits to a cell count separately.
\item \textbf{Rates with absorption.}  The table holds the pure Thomson rates; a
  walker with an absorption opacity forms its rates, its conversion probability and
  its force from the total extinction at its energy (Section~\ref{sec:rates}).
\item \textbf{Threshold.}  \key{tau} defaults to 3 and is refused below 2, where
  $P(1)$ exceeds 1 (Section~\ref{sec:interface}); a clamped probability is counted.
\item \textbf{Report.}  Cells, dwells, scatterings stood for, hops and hops through
  block faces, conversions at faces and at flight start, returns, emergences, releases,
  budget stops, clamps; \key{report} also turns on the histogram of analog
  scatterings by cell depth.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{What the tests found.}  The conversion probability was the one error of the
draft (Section~\ref{sec:interface}), and only the pair of tests separated it from the
lattice's discretization: the Thomson sphere, whose interface shell of cut cells is
transport, escaped 5 to 13 percent too fast at every resolution, while the cube that
fills the mesh, where the lattice has no interface, reproduced its exact first-passage
solution (mean path and dwell count) to the statistics.  With the factor corrected the
sphere agrees with the analog run in the mean escape path to 0.5 percent at 16 and 32
cells across for cell depths from 2 to 16, and to 2 to 3 percent at 8 cells across
whatever the depth (the blocky sphere and its shell), the escape-path distributions
within the two-sample Kolmogorov scale.  Free-free absorption, monochromatic and so
grey, agrees under the weight method to $3\times10^{-4}$ in the escaping weight at an
absorbed fraction of 18 percent and under the probability method within the binomial
error; a case where 99.8 percent is absorbed, an exponential of the path, converges
from 30 to 6 percent between 16 and 32 cells.  In the cube's moments (depth 5), the
energy density agrees per cell to 5 percent with the lattice's expected shape (4
percent high at the source, 3 to 6 low at the boundary), the flux and the force to a
percent on average with the luminosity through the inner shells conserved, and
$\chi F/c$ equals the deposited force to half a percent in both runs; the off-diagonal
pressure is zero on the lattice.  A dwell costs 0.24 to 1 microseconds against 0.4 to
0.5 for a scattering, so the gain is the scatterings a dwell stands for: 100 times at
depth 10, 7 to 19 times on the gridded spheres at depths 5 to 8.

The disk atmosphere of \texttt{tst/montecarlo/disk\_atmosphere} (spherical polar, the
legacy pusher, polarized, free-free emission and absorption, cells up to 760 deep at
the midplane; the lattice takes 26 of its 131 thousand cells at a threshold of 3):
the ladder of 1, 4 and 16 million photons passes the Feautrier gate with the lattice
as without it (grazing $Q/I = -0.0395 \pm 0.0090$ against $-0.0401$, the norm falling as
$N^{-0.49}$), the emergent intensities agree bin by bin within their errors, and in
the moments (4 intervals of 500 thousand photons, the error from the scatter of the
interval sums, which carries the correlations along a photon's path) the energy
density and the vertical flux summed over the thin layers agree to 1 to 2 percent
within errors of 2.5 percent, the deep energy density to about a percent.  The deep
fluxes and forces are noise in both runs: where absorption dominates, a photon dies
after a scattering or two while a hop carries $\chi\Delta/2$ times a scattering's
momentum, so the lattice's force estimator is the noisier there by orders of
magnitude, a property to keep in mind for radiation forces in absorption-dominated
thick cells.  It was this test that showed the rates needed the total extinction.

\section{What D2 built, where it departs from Section~\ref{sec:energy}}
\label{sec:d2}

Built 2026-10-29 on branch \texttt{ddmc}, in \texttt{ddmc.cpp} with one check lifted in
the constructor.  The dwell under \key{scattering = compton} draws the walker's energy
from the Kompaneets table at $y = \theta\,N$, with $N$ the scatterings the dwell stands
for (the walker's scattering opacity times $ct$), in chunks of the table's $y_{\max}$
with a fresh draw per chunk (the process is Markov in $y$ at fixed $\theta$), and its
free-free survival from the same table with \texttt{lam} rescaled to the walker's $y$;
any other absorption multiplies by $\exp(-\kappa_a ct)$.  The opacities are refreshed at
the new energy, the source terms take the energy change as they take the absorbed
weight, and the moments use the mean of the energies before and after.  The table file
is \key{kgreens\_file} in \texttt{<ddmc>} or \texttt{<randomwalk>}.  Two counters were
added: conversions declined because the photon's energy fails the guards, and walkers
released because a dwell took their energy outside the table; the report prints both
under Compton.

Two departures from Section~\ref{sec:energy}, both forced by the Compton sphere.
First, there is no bound on $x\theta$.  With the random walk's $x\theta\le0.05$, at
$\theta = 0.01$ the lattice came out 6 percent low in the mean escaping energy with the
spectrum empty above $x = 5$: an energy at which walkers are released and transport
photons are not converted is a boundary in energy between dwells that stand for about
twenty scatterings and single scatterings, and a random walk with uneven steps across a
line depletes the side with the longer steps.  With the bound at $x\theta\le0.1$ the
mean energy agreed with the Kompaneets theory to 0.1 percent, so the lattice keeps only
the table's $x$ range, which for the cells that $\theta\le0.03$ admits lies far out in
the Wien tail, and the free-free sink guard.  Second, the leakage rates, the conversion
probability and $y$ take the walker's scattering opacity, the thermally averaged
Klein-Nishina value that the analog run uses, through the per-walker rates
(\texttt{DDMCRates}, \texttt{DDMCNeighbourExtinction}): with the Thomson rates the
lattice reproduced the Thomson diffusion theory's escape paths exactly while the analog
run's were 7 to 12 percent shorter at $\theta$ of 0.01 to 0.02, and its spectrum lacked
the hardening that the energy-dependent escape gives.  The cell guard $\theta\le0.03$ is
applied in \texttt{BuildDDMCRates}, so such cells are transport cells.

Measurements, the Compton sphere of \texttt{sphere\_compton.py} with $10^5$ photons, the
lattice against the driver's Green's-function gate and against the analog run of the
same deck ($x_0 = 1$, $\theta = 0.002$ unless said): the gate passes on the lattice as
on the analog run at $\tau$ of 15 (8 cells across, 57 diffusion cells), 40 (8 and 16
cells) and 100 (32 cells, 7249 diffusion cells), at $x_0$ of 0.1 and 3, and with
free-free absorption at a radius where it takes 14 percent (escaping weight 0.8606
against 0.8602 analog and 0.8579 theory at $x_0 = 0.3$, 0.9742 against 0.9741 at
$x_0 = 1$); the lattice and the analog spectra agree within 0.5 to 1.8 times the
two-sample Kolmogorov scale and the mean escaping energies to a percent, the escape
paths within 2 times the scale at 16 and 32 cells and 6 times at 8 cells (the coarse
lattice's short paths of Section~\ref{sec:d1}).  At $\theta$ of 0.01 and 0.02 with
both departures built, the lattice agrees with the analog run to 0.5 percent in the
mean energy and 1 to 1.4 times the scale in the spectrum, while both sit 2 to 3 percent
above the Thomson Kompaneets theory, which is the exact kernel's hardening and not the
lattice's.  At $\tau = 100$ on 32 cells the analog run's $4.8\times10^8$ scatterings
become $4.8\times10^6$ scatterings and $1.6\times10^7$ dwells.  The driver's own
free-free survival check fails identically on the analog and the lattice at the default
radius of $10^{10}$ cm, where the sink is $10^{-4}$: a deck limitation, which the radius
of $10^7$ cm used here avoids.

\section{What D4 built, and the two attempts the moving sphere rejected}
\label{sec:d4}

Built 2026-10-30.  The test is the Compton sphere of D2 (tau 40 over 16 cells, cells 5
deep, $x_0 = 1$, $\theta = 2\times10^{-3}$, $10^5$ photons) with the fluid moving:
uniformly along $z$ through the fixed escape surface (\texttt{--beta}), and in a
homologous expansion with the given velocity at the surface
(\texttt{--profile homologous}, added to \texttt{mc\_sphere\_isoth} and the driver), the
lattice against the analog run of the same deck.  The interface-free cube of
\texttt{ddmc\_cube.py} takes the same two flows (\texttt{--beta}, \texttt{--profile}),
which is what told the lattice's interior from its interface.

\textbf{What the design note had, measured.}  The hop transform through a $W(\mu)$
direction and a walker at rest in the lab: in the uniform flow at $\beta = 0.1$ the
escape paths were 2.1 times the analog's and the mean escaping energy 12 percent high,
at $\beta = 0.3$ 5.4 times and 82 percent; in the expansion 1.8 and 3.1 times.  The
lattice kept the static residence time whatever the flow, since nothing carried the
walkers.  A scratch build with the hop transform switched off moved the expansion's
energy by 9 percent ($\beta = 0.1$) and 36 percent ($\beta = 0.3$), the size of that
transform, in the wrong direction and far short of the analog's cooling.

\textbf{First attempt: drift at the cell-centre velocity, the divergence term, no hop
transform.}  Uniform flow right to 5 percent in the paths (1 percent at $\beta = 0.3$)
and 2 percent in the energy; the expansion still 21 percent slow to escape and 6
percent cold at $\beta = 0.1$.  Interpolating the drift velocity across the cell
(so that the face velocity is the mean of the two cells', as the analog's one-way
fluxes make it) took the expansion to 16 percent.  The cube with the same flows, no
interface, was then 8 percent (uniform) and 18 percent (expansion) slow: the error was
in the interior.  The cause was the hop placing the walker at the neighbour's centre:
a drifting walker sits on average in the downstream half of its cell, so every hop set
it back by a quarter of a cell.  A hop that keeps the walker's offset (a translation by
one cell) made the cube agree in the mean path to 0.5 percent in both flows and in
the mean energy to 0.3 percent (uniform) and 1.5 percent (expansion).

\textbf{Second: the interface in the lab frame.}  With the interior right, the sphere's
paths agreed to 1 percent at every velocity, but the energies were 3 percent low in the
uniform flow and 8 percent (14 percent at $\beta = 0.3$) in the expansion: the
conversion test read the photon's cosine and energy in the lab and returned a photon
isotropic in the lab.  Taking the test into the diffusion cell's frame (transform in,
decide, convert with the energy already comoving or transform the inward direction
out) moved the energies by 5 percent at $\beta = 0.1$.

\textbf{Result.}  Against the analog run, median escape path and mean escaping energy,
lattice over analog, and the spectrum's Kolmogorov distance in units of the two-sample
scale (0.6 to 1.2 for the static lattice at this resolution):
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llccc}
flow & $\beta$ & path & energy & spectrum \\
\hline
uniform & 0.01 & 0.991 & 1.000 & 0.9 \\
uniform & 0.1 & 1.002 & 1.017 & 2.6 \\
uniform & 0.3 & 1.008 & 1.028 & 4.5 \\
expansion & 0.1 & 1.007 & 0.966 & 4.3 \\
expansion & 0.3 & 1.012 & 0.893 & 11.8 \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The residence time is right everywhere.  The energy is right to 2 to 3 percent at
$\beta = 0.1$, with opposite signs in the two flows, and the expansion at $\beta = 0.3$
is 11 percent cold: what is left is first order in $\beta$ at the interface (the
emergence placed a hair inside the cell and flown out, the returned photon's
distribution, the advective emergence's hemisphere) and the within-cell velocity
gradient the drift's interpolation only partly represents.  The static sphere, the
cube's exact check and the D3 block, repack and refinement tests are unchanged.

\textbf{What this means for the design.}  Three statements of Section~\ref{sec:moving}
as first written were wrong and are replaced there: the hop transform is not a
physical shift for a diffusing walker (it double counts the adiabatic loss by
$1.42/\tau_{\rm cell}$ and misses the rest); the advection cannot be left out at
$\beta\tau_{\rm cell}$ of order one, where it sets the residence time; and the walker is
not at the cell's centre, only where it entered as a photon.  The random walk's
advection room, which declines steps that would leave the cell, has no counterpart:
the walker crosses.


\section{R0: the resonance line, design note}
\label{sec:r0}

Written 2026-10-30 for approval before R1, approved the same day.  The target is Lyman alpha in
\texttt{mc\_hotjupiter}; the method is the frequency lattice of Smith et al.\ (2018),
their Section 2, carried onto the lattice of Sections~\ref{sec:rates} to
\ref{sec:moving} as built in D1 to D4, with the changes that D4 forces on their
treatment of a moving medium.  Equation numbers below are theirs.

\subsection{What exists, and the scope}

The analog transport has the line already: \texttt{XsecVoigt} (the Voigt cross section
at the photon's comoving frequency, Lyman alpha constants in \texttt{MCConstants}),
\texttt{ResonanceLineOpacity} ($n_{\rm H}$ from \texttt{species(0)} times it),
\texttt{ScatterResonanceLine} (the partially coherent kernel: parallel atom velocity by
the rejection sampler of \texttt{SampleVelocityParallel}, perpendicular Maxwellian,
dipole phase function; no recoil), the uniform sphere \texttt{mc\_sphere\_lya} with a
point source at \key{x0}, \key{tau} and a uniform \key{velocity}, and in
\texttt{mc\_hotjupiter} the same opacity and kernel enrolled as user functions
(\texttt{scattering = user}, \texttt{absorption = user}, the line switched off beyond
\key{linewidth\_cutoff}), the fluid-derived $n_{\rm H}$ from the neutral scalar and the
temperature from the ionization state, and a core-skipping hook
(\texttt{CoreSkipping}, \texttt{UserWorkInMove}) that moves a photon with
$|x| < 3.3$ to $x = \pm3.3$ whenever its cell is thicker than 1000 at its frequency.
The Lyman alpha decks (\texttt{~/mc\_test/hot\_jupiter/lya\_old}, \texttt{combo}) run
the legacy spherical-polar pusher (\texttt{general\_pusher = false}) and are static
(\texttt{dynamic = false}, a wind snapshot post-processed); the coupled ionization runs
are dynamic and use the general pusher.  The legacy sphere step of
\texttt{PhotonPusher::MRWResonanceAcceleration} (Dijkstra's frequency solution,
spherical polar only, threshold 1000) is what R2 retires.

Scope: Lyman alpha only (the constants are the module's), one line, no dust, the
analog kernel unchanged for transport photons, the lattice in flat space on the two
legacy pushers.  The frequency lattice is a second axis of the D1 lattice, not a new
mechanism: a node is a (cell, bin), a dwell is drawn at the node's total leakage, and
the face chosen is one of six spatial faces or two frequency faces.

\subsection{The frequency grid}

Frequencies are $x = (\nu - \nu_0)/\Delta\nu_D$ with $\Delta\nu_D = \nu_0 v_{\rm th}/c$,
$v_{\rm th} = (2kT/m_{\rm H})^{1/2}$, and the damping parameter $a =
\Delta\nu_L/2\Delta\nu_D = 4.70\times10^{-4}\,(T/10^4\,{\rm K})^{-1/2}$, as in the code.
The cells of the hot Jupiter are at 3000 to 7000 K, so a grid in the $x$ of each cell
would put the same bin on different frequencies in neighbouring cells, and a spatial
hop would have to rescale.  The grid is therefore \textbf{one grid of comoving
frequencies for the whole mesh}: bin edges $\nu_j$ set once from a reference
temperature, \key{line\_temp}, in whose Doppler units the keys are read: \key{xmax}
(the grid spans $|x| \le$ \key{xmax}), \key{dx} (the wing bin width, 1 by default: a
wing scattering shifts $x$ by one on average, Osterbrock 1962, so narrower bins resolve
nothing the Fokker-Planck form describes) and \key{core\_width} (the half-width of the
central bin, default 3.3, Smith's $\Delta x_0 \gtrsim 2x_{\rm cw}$ and the hot
Jupiter's core-skip boundary).  Each cell sees the grid through its own $T$: its bin
$j$ spans $[x_j^-, x_j^+]$ in its own Doppler units, its bin-averaged extinction is
\begin{equation}
k_{i,j} = \frac{n_{\rm H}\,\sigma_0}{\sqrt{\pi}\,\Delta\nu_D}\;
\frac{\mathcal{H}(a, x_j^+) - \mathcal{H}(a, x_j^-)}{x_j^+ - x_j^-},\qquad
\mathcal{H}(a,x) = \int_0^x H(a,y)\,\dd y
\approx \frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\,{\rm erf}\,x - \frac{2a}{\sqrt\pi}F(x) + a^2 x\,e^{-x^2},
\label{eq:kbin}
\end{equation}
their equations 13 and 14 with $\sigma_0 = \pi e^2 f/m_e c$ and $F$ the Dawson
integral ($F(x) = -\tfrac12\,{\rm Re}\,Z(x)$ from the \texttt{ZetaVoigt} the code has;
the expansion is second order in $a$, and $a \sim 6\times10^{-4}$ here).  This is the
extinction the lattice uses for the node and for the spatial rates; the transport
photon keeps the exact Voigt value at its own frequency.

A node $(i,j)$ is a diffusion node when $k_{i,j}\,\Delta_{\rm min} \ge$ \key{tau}, the
same key and the same smallest-width rule as the grey lattice, tested over the ghost
cells as in Section~\ref{sec:faces}.  Since $k_{i,j}$ falls as $a/\sqrt\pi x^2$ in the
wings, the lattice of a cell is a contiguous band $|x| \le x_{\rm edge}(i)$ around line
centre, widest where $n_{\rm H}$ is highest; the base of the hot Jupiter atmosphere
($\tau_0 \sim 10^5$ across a cell) has $x_{\rm edge} \approx 20$ at threshold 3, and
the band closes as the wind ionizes.  \textbf{Storage}: $k_{i,j}$ for every (cell, bin)
and the node flag, $9\,n_{\rm bin}$ bytes per cell ($n_{\rm bin} = 2\,$\key{xmax}$/$\key{dx}
$+ 1$); 80 bins on the hot Jupiter's $128\times33\times33$ mesh is 100 MB over all
ranks.  The rates are formed per dwell from $k_{i,j}$ and the neighbours' values, as
\texttt{DDMCRates} already forms the grey ones per walker, so nothing else is stored.
The divergence and widths of the grey table stay per cell.

\subsection{Rates and the dwell}

Per unit comoving path, for a walker at node $(i,j)$:
\begin{align}
k^{\delta i}_{\rm space} &= \frac{2}{3\Delta_i\,(k_{i,j}\Delta_i + k_{\delta i,j}\Delta_{\delta i})}
\quad\text{into a diffusion neighbour in the same bin,}\qquad
\frac{2}{3\Delta_i\,(k_{i,j}\Delta_i + 2\lambda)}\quad\text{otherwise;}
\label{eq:kspace}\\
k^{\delta j}_{\rm freq} &= \frac{1}{\Delta x_j}\;\frac{1}{k_{i,j}^{-1}\Delta x_j + k_{i,\delta j}^{-1}\Delta x_{\delta j}}
\quad\text{into either neighbouring bin (their equation 11),}
\label{eq:kfreq}
\end{align}
with $\Delta x$ in the cell's own Doppler units.  Equation~\ref{eq:kspace} is the D1
rate (Section~\ref{sec:rates}) with $k_{i,j}$ in place of the Thomson extinction;
equation~\ref{eq:kfreq} is the finite-volume form of the Fokker-Planck term
$\partial_x(\tfrac{k}{2}\partial_x J)$, the harmonic mean of the two bins'
extinctions.  A frequency face at the grid's edge ($|x| = $ \key{xmax}) and a
frequency face into a bin that is not a diffusion node in this cell are boundary faces
of the lattice in frequency: the walker emerges through them (below).  The total is
the sum of the six spatial and two frequency rates, the dwell is exponential at the
total, capped by the budget and by the drift of Section~\ref{sec:moving} exactly as
now, and the face is drawn in proportion.  Absorption (the hot Jupiter's user
function, zero for the line) acts over the dwell as in Section~\ref{sec:energy}; the
scatterings the dwell stands for, $k_{i,j}\,ct$, go to \texttt{nscp} and the report.

\subsection{The walker's frequency}

The walker carries its comoving energy \texttt{ep} as now; its bin is read from
\texttt{ep} and its cell's $T$ whenever it arrives in a cell.  Inside a node the
frequency is not followed: the bin is the state.  Three rules, all settled by D4:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A spatial hop keeps \texttt{ep}.}  The comoving frequency, measured from
  the local line centre, is continuous across a face (Section~\ref{sec:moving}); the
  Doppler correction of their equations 19 and 20 (a relabelling by $-\Delta
  \boldsymbol{\beta}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}$ with a mean cosine $f_\mu$, which they call
  the weak point of their treatment) is not applied, for the reason given there and
  measured in Section~\ref{sec:d4}.  The bin is recomputed on arrival from the
  neighbour's $T$, which is the temperature rescaling they apply.
\item \textbf{The velocity gradient acts over the dwell}, as the adiabatic term of
  Section~\ref{sec:moving}: $\dd\ln\nu = -\tfrac13\nabla\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol{\beta}\,
  \dd(ct)$, applied to \texttt{ep}, after which the bin is recomputed; a walker whose
  bin changed by the drift is treated as having crossed that frequency face (a hop
  into the neighbouring node if it is a diffusion node, an emergence otherwise), with
  no deposit.  In the hot Jupiter wind, $\nabla\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol{v} \sim v/r \sim
  2\times10^{-9}\,{\rm s^{-1}}$ over a trapping time of $10^5$ s moves a photon by
  about two Doppler widths to the red: a real effect, and the only velocity effect the
  lattice interior has.  The dwell need not be cut at the bin edge as the spatial
  drift is: the drift per dwell is far below a bin.
\item \textbf{Emergence draws the frequency uniformly in the bin it leaves into} (a
  spatial emergence: the walker's own bin; a frequency emergence: the thin bin or the
  bin beyond \key{xmax}), with the direction from $W(\mu)$ for a spatial face and
  isotropic for a frequency face, at the walker's position; then
  \texttt{TransformToCoordinate} as now.  For the core bin this is complete
  redistribution over the core at emergence.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The interface}

Spatial faces are D1's: a transport photon reaching the face of a cell in which its
bin is a diffusion node converts with $P(\mu)$ of Section~\ref{sec:interface},
evaluated in the diffusion cell's frame with $k_{i,j}$, or returns; a walker leaking
into a cell where its bin is not a node emerges.  Frequency interfaces need no
probability: a transport photon enters the lattice in frequency \textbf{by
scattering}: the analog kernel redistributes it, and if the new $x$ lies in a
diffusion node of its cell it converts at the start of its next flight, the rule
\texttt{DDMCBeforeFlight} already applies to a photon that scattered in a diffusion
cell.  Since the kernel is exact, this entry is unbiased; the lattice's own
approximation begins only inside.  (R1 found otherwise: the entry needs the
probability of equation~\ref{eq:pfreq}, Section~\ref{sec:r1}.)  A walker leaves the lattice in frequency by the
leakage of equation~\ref{eq:kfreq} into a thin bin (emergence, frequency drawn in
that bin).  Smith et al.\ (their Section 5.1) leave the frequency hybrid open and
note that a central bin that blocks leakage across line centre cannot reproduce the
red/blue peak asymmetry of a flow; here the core bin spans line centre and a walker in
it leaks to either side by equation~\ref{eq:kfreq} with the core's own $k_{i,0}$,
which is their scheme and has the same limitation.  R2 measures it.

The core-skipping hook of \texttt{mc\_hotjupiter} runs on every photon that
\texttt{Move} returns, walkers included (the dwell calls \texttt{UserWorkInMove}); it
must not touch a walker.  R3 runs the hot Jupiter with \key{core\_skip = false}, the
lattice's core bin doing that job, and the hook is left for the analog comparison run.

\subsection{What the Fokker-Planck form drops, and whether it matters here}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Core redistribution.}  Complete redistribution in the core is not
  diffusion in $x$; the core bin of width $2\,$\key{core\_width} hides it, and a walker
  emerging from the core is redistributed uniformly over it.  Their slab test (their
  figure 2) shows the emergent line of a $10^9$-depth slab reproduced to the plotted
  precision with this; R1 repeats that test.
\item \textbf{Recoil}, $g = h\Delta\nu_D/2kT \approx 3\times10^{-4}$ at 7000 K, a
  redward shift of $g$ per scattering against a random walk of unit steps: it competes
  with the diffusion only after $g^{-2} \sim 10^7$ scatterings, $a\tau_0 \gtrsim
  10^4$.  The analog kernel has no recoil either, so lattice and analog agree with
  each other; neither is right there, and this is recorded, not fixed.
\item \textbf{Detailed balance and stimulated emission}: dropped by both, negligible
  for Lyman alpha at these temperatures.
\item \textbf{Partial coherence in the wing}: the Fokker-Planck form keeps the drift
  $-1/x$ and the unit variance per scattering of the wing kernel, which is all the
  diffusion limit uses.
\item \textbf{The velocity gradient within a cell}: as in Section~\ref{sec:moving},
  the isotropic average only.  The hot Jupiter's wind is slow against the thermal
  width within a cell ($\Delta v$ across a cell below $v_{\rm th}$ everywhere but the
  outer wind, where the lattice has no nodes), so the lattice's velocity treatment is
  the dwell term alone, and the comparison that decides is the expanding sphere of R2.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Decisions for the user}
\label{sec:r0decisions}

All six approved as proposed on 2026-10-30; Section~\ref{sec:r1} is the build.
\begin{enumerate}
\item One comoving-frequency grid for the mesh, set by \key{line\_temp}, against
  Smith's per-cell Doppler grid with rescaling at hops.  Proposed: the one grid.
\item The hop keeps the comoving frequency and the velocity gradient acts over the
  dwell (D4), against their equations 19 and 20.  Proposed: D4's rule; R2 is the
  measurement.
\item Entry into the lattice in frequency by scattering, with no interface
  probability at frequency faces.  Proposed as written.  (Built with a probability,
  Section~\ref{sec:r1}: without one the spectra were 5 to 7 percent low.)
\item The core bin of Smith (complete redistribution at emergence, leakage by the
  Fokker-Planck rate with the core's own extinction) rather than a redistribution
  matrix (their Section 5.3, which they estimate at terabytes for a general table).
  Proposed: the core bin, the asymmetry measured in R2.
\item The hot Jupiter Lyman alpha runs stay on the legacy spherical-polar pusher.
  If a production run must use the general pusher, the flat-space part of D5 comes
  before R3.
\item Where the code lives: the bin tables, the bin opacity and the frequency rates in
  a new \texttt{ddmc\_line.cpp}; the walk, hop and emergence of \texttt{ddmc.cpp}
  take a node (cell, bin) in place of a cell and two more faces.
\end{enumerate}

\section{What R1 built, where it departs from the R0 note}
\label{sec:r1}

Built 2026-10-30 after the six decisions of Section~\ref{sec:r0decisions} were
approved as proposed.  The code is \texttt{ddmc\_line.cpp} (the grid, the bin-averaged
extinction, the per-cell node band, the frequency rates, the frequency hop, emergence
and entry) over \texttt{ddmc.cpp}, which now asks one membership test,
\texttt{DDMCIsNode(cell, energy)}, wherever it read the cell mask, and takes the
walker's extinction from the bin under the line; the pushers are unchanged.  The
refactor of \texttt{ddmc.cpp} was held bitwise against the pre-R1 binary on the grey
runs (the cube with a homologous flow, the refined multi-block sphere) before the line
code went in, and the finished line code leaves those runs bitwise too.

\textbf{Where it departs from Section~\ref{sec:r0}.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The frequency interface has a conversion probability}, against decision
  3.  A transport photon that scatters from a bin that is not a node of its cell into
  a wing node converts with
  \begin{equation}
  P_x = \frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{2\,\Delta x_j},
  \label{eq:pfreq}
  \end{equation}
  $\Delta x_j$ the node bin's width in the cell's Doppler units, and is otherwise
  reflected about the bin edge it crossed (\texttt{DDMCLineEntry}, called from the
  scattering loop with the comoving energy before the scattering); an entry into the
  core bin, whose kernel redistributes over the core at the next scattering, converts
  with probability 1, as does a photon already at a node (one just emitted there).  The
  value balances the lattice's leakage out of the edge bin for a uniform population
  $J$ per unit $x$, $J k/(2\Delta x_j)$ by equation~\ref{eq:kfreq}, against the kernel's
  crossings of the edge from the same population, $J k\,E[\max(\Delta x, 0)] =
  Jk/\sqrt{2\pi}$ for its unit-variance steps (the wing kernel's step is a mixture of
  Gaussians of variance $1 - \cos\theta$, whose one-sided mean is 0.376 against the
  Gaussian's 0.399, a difference left out).  It is the frequency counterpart of
  $P(\mu)$ at a spatial face, without the extrapolation-length term, which is of
  relative order $1/\Delta x_j$ and small on the bins used (10 to 45); at $\Delta x_j
  \le 1.25$ it is 1, the R0 rule.
\item \textbf{A frequency emergence puts the photon at the edge it crossed}, plus the
  outward overshoot of a scattering step (a uniform fraction of a Rayleigh step of
  unit variance), not uniformly in the thin bin; that is where a kinetic crossing
  from a uniform population lands, and the pair of rules keeps the thin side in
  equilibrium with a uniform lattice population.  A spatial emergence still draws the
  frequency uniformly in the walker's own bin, the bin being the state.
\item \textbf{A virtual bin beyond each end of the grid.}  The stored grid has
  $n_{\rm bin} + 2$ bins per cell: the $2\,n_{\rm wing} + 1$ bins of the note and one
  bin of width \key{dx} beyond \key{xmax} on each side, never a node, whose
  extinction gives the edge bin its outer frequency rate.  $n_{\rm wing} =
  \lceil(\key{xmax} - \key{core\_width})/\key{dx}\rceil$, so the grid's edge is at or
  beyond \key{xmax}.
\item \textbf{The node band is stored as its edge}, the offset of the outermost node
  bin from the core on either side (\texttt{ddmc\_line\_edge}, $-1$ when not even the
  core is a node), since $k_{i,j}$ falls away from the core.  A cell is a diffusion
  cell (\texttt{ddmc\_cell}) when its core is a node, and the grey code's test of the
  cell comes first everywhere, the bin test after it.
\item \textbf{A frequency hop draws the frequency uniformly in the new bin}, as the
  walker's energy must lie in its bin for the bin to be read from it; the note kept
  \texttt{ep} across a frequency hop.  A spatial hop keeps \texttt{ep}, as in the note.
\item \textbf{The Dawson integral} comes from \texttt{ZetaVoigt} only for $|x| \le 6$;
  beyond, from its asymptotic series ($\sum_n (2n-1)!!/2^{n+1}x^{2n+1}$, eight terms).
  \texttt{ZetaVoigt} is accurate to $10^{-7}$ absolutely, which is the size of the
  difference $\mathcal{H}(x^+) - \mathcal{H}(x^-)$ of a far-wing bin.  Checked against
  quadrature: $F$ to $10^{-9}$, $\mathcal{H}$ to $10^{-10}$ at $a = 4.7\times10^{-4}$
  and $8\times10^{-7}$ at $a = 0.015$, a wing bin at $x = 1000$ against $a/\sqrt\pi
  x^2$ to $1.4\times10^{-6}$.
\item \textbf{The walker's extinction is the bin's}: \texttt{scp} is $k_{i,j}$ of its
  bin, reset at every frequency hop, and the rates are formed per dwell from it and
  the neighbours' bin extinctions; the D1 rate table is not used under the line, and
  its \texttt{chi} is the core bin's $k$ so that the report's depth histogram and the
  level-jump counts read as before.
\item \textbf{The scattering count saturates} at \texttt{INT\_MAX}: a dwell at
  $a\tau_0 = 10^7$ stands for $10^8$ scatterings, and an overflowed \texttt{nscp} would
  have stopped the conversion after a scattering (the test \texttt{nscp > 0}).
\item \textbf{The analog kernel's parallel-velocity sampler}
  (\texttt{SampleVelocityParallel}) took 94 percent of a lattice slab run: its
  rejection scheme used Zheng and Miralda-Escud\'e's $u_0 = 0.96\,x$ at every $|x|$,
  whose acceptance falls as $3/x^2$, 1900 uniform draws per scattering at $|x| = 40$,
  where every emerged photon of a thick run scatters.  It now uses the $u_0(x, a)$ of
  Laursen et al.\ (2009) as written by Smith et al.\ (2015): $u_0 = 0$ for $x < 0.2$,
  $u_0 = x - 0.01\,a^{1/6}e^{1.2x}$ up to $x_{\rm cw} = 1.59 - 0.60\log_{10}a -
  0.03\log_{10}^2 a$, and $u_0 = 4.5$ beyond, with the acceptance $\sim 1.5/x$.  The
  scheme is exact for any $u_0$; the analog slab at $\tau_0 = 10^5$ with 1000 photons
  is statistically unchanged (two-sample distances of $x$ and of the path at 0.7 and
  0.65 of their scale).  This touches every resonance run, the hot Jupiter included.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{How the interface rule was found.}  With the R0 rule (every entry converts)
the escaping spectra were 5 to 7 percent low in the median ($-0.28$ bins, Kolmogorov
distance 0.09 to 0.10 against a sampling scale of 0.016) exactly where the node band
ends on the peak of the spectrum (the slab at $a\tau_0 = 10^6$ over 32 cells, the
sphere at $10^5$, the scan's 32-cell slab), and within 0.1 bin where the band ends
well beyond the peak (the escape happens from inside the lattice) or well inside it
(the peak is analog).  Equation~\ref{eq:pfreq} with the emergence still uniform in the
thin bin overshot the other way ($+0.4$ to $+0.5$ bins, 0.12).  The toy
\texttt{freq\_interface\_toy.py} separated the two rules: a diffusion in $x$ on
$[0, L]$ with the lattice below $x_e$ and a Gaussian random walk above, born at 0 and
absorbed at $L$, its mean absorption time against the exact $(L^2 - x_0^2)/2D$ at
$L = 120$, $x_e = 60$ and $\Delta x = 10$, 5, 2.5: converting every crossing gives
1.36, 1.31, 1.18 of the exact time; equation~\ref{eq:pfreq} with uniform placement
0.86, 0.87, 0.91; equation~\ref{eq:pfreq} with the overshoot placement 1.045, 1.022,
1.019.  Only the last converges.

\textbf{The test medium.}  \texttt{mc\_sphere\_lya} first kept the lattice to the
cells wholly inside the escape surface, as \texttt{mc\_sphere\_isoth} does; at a cell

\section{What R2 built: the moving line, and the stub retired}
\label{sec:r2}

Built 2026-10-30.  The moving line needed almost no new code: D4's drift and
adiabatic shift already act on a walker's comoving energy, and R1 treats a shift across
a bin edge as a frequency crossing with nothing deposited.  The build is one rule, one
removal and the measurements.

\textbf{No adiabatic shift in the core bin.}  A walker's energy loses
$\exp(-ct\,\nabla\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol\beta/3)$ over a dwell in a wing bin but not in
the core bin.  A scattering in the Doppler core draws the comoving frequency afresh
(the resonant atom's velocity is the photon's, and the re-emitted $x' \approx
x\cos\theta' + u_\perp\sin\theta'\cos\phi'$ has no memory of the drift accumulated
along the flight), so the shift the analog photon gains between core scatterings is
erased at each one and nothing accumulates over a core dwell; in the wing the
scattering is coherent in the atom's frame and the shift is kept.  With the shift
applied in the core the lattice was 8 percent redder than the analog run at a surface
velocity of one Doppler width (red fraction 0.80 against 0.72) and its paths 8 to 11
percent short; without it 4 percent and 4 to 5 percent.

\textbf{\texttt{MRWResonanceAcceleration} removed}, with its escape-time sampler and
the moment hook \texttt{UpdateMomentsAcceleration} that only it called: the random
walk's resonance step of the general pusher, a single-sphere escape with a fixed
threshold of 1000 that never reached the legacy pushers the Lyman alpha runs use.
\key{<randomwalk> enable} with \key{scattering = resonance} is now refused at start-up
with a message naming the frequency lattice.  The grey runs stay bitwise.

\textbf{Frame bookkeeping verified apart from the drift.}  A uniform flow along $z$
at three Doppler widths has every Doppler transform of a moving medium (at each
scattering, conversion and emergence, and at the escape) and no velocity divergence:
the lattice against the analog run on the sphere at $\tau_0 = 10^6$ and 10 K, 2000
photons, agrees in the escaping frequencies within 1.2 times the two-sample scale and
in the paths within 1.0 and 0.5 at 16 and 32 cells across, with the red fraction and the
mean $x$ matching.  The static control at 4000 photons: frequencies within 0.8 and 0.9
scales, paths 1.8 and 1.2 (the lattice's mean path 4.5 percent long at both resolutions,
the core bin's residence by the harmonic exit rate, which the spectrum does not

spatial rule, is redder (0.05, three scales; the same in the code, which is why the
hop keeps the uniform redraw); no reflection at the interface and an emergence
exactly at the edge are worse; testing the entries that the drift carries in, and
halving or doubling the probability, move the numbers within the toy's noise.  The
departure at a moderate drift is therefore the lattice's own, of the order of the
discretization at this bin width, and not a code error; the mean shift $-1/x$ of the
wing kernel is what makes the kinetic process the diffusion the rates discretize, and
a toy without it disagrees with the lattice even at rest.

\textbf{Judgement.}  The lattice carries a moving line: a strong expansion
(ten Doppler widths at the surface) to the sampling scale in the spectrum, a weak one
(one Doppler width) with the red fraction high by 0.01 to 0.02 and the mean $x$ redder
by 1 to 1.5 Doppler widths out of 13, and the escape paths within 5 percent at the
resolution whose band covers the peak.  The hot Jupiter's lattice region is the dense
base of the wind, where the flow is below the thermal width, so R3 sits in the weak
regime; the error there is of the size of the lattice's static discretization at 32
cells, and smaller than the difference between the two analog resolutions of the
scan.  The red/blue peak asymmetry is carried by the drift in the wing bins and the
erasure in the core, which is the mechanism Smith et al.\ could not reproduce with a
core bin that blocks leakage across line centre; here the core bin does not block it,
and the asymmetry comes out right to the accuracy above.

\section{What R3 built: spherical polar, the polar axis, and the core's kinetic exit}
\label{sec:r3}

Built 2026-10-31.  The hot Jupiter is a wind in spherical polar coordinates whose
lattice region, the dense base, holds a node band that is the core bin alone, and the
phase turned out to be mostly about those two facts.  The hot Jupiter measurements
close the section.

\textbf{The lattice in spherical polar coordinates.}  D1's leakage rates and face
geometry already took the spherical pusher's cell widths; the moving medium of D4 did
not.  The dwell's loss now carries the geometric terms of the divergence,
$2\beta_r/r + \cot\theta\,\beta_\theta/r$, and the drift converts the comoving
displacement into coordinate steps with the scale factors $r$ along $\theta$ and
$r\sin\theta$ along $\phi$ at the cell's centre.  Two guards came with it: a face of
a direction the mesh does not resolve (one cell, periodic or reflecting) is no
interface, since the medium beyond it is the same cell, and the node test refuses an
index beyond the array in such a direction.  \texttt{mc\_sphere\_lya} gained the
geometry: the medium fills a spherical polar mesh, the source is a shell at the inner
edge, isotropic outward, which the reflecting inner boundary makes the point source as
the inner radius goes to zero, the escape is the outer boundary, and a move hook keeps
the path and the scattering count in the user columns; \texttt{sphere\_lya.py
--checks rsphere} (the binary of \texttt{--path-spherical}, \texttt{--ntheta} cells
in $\theta$ and $\phi$) runs it against Dijkstra et al., and
\texttt{--geometry-moving rsphere} runs the moving check there.

\textbf{The polar axis.}  A $\theta$ face on the axis has no area, and the lattice
had treated it as a face: it leaked through it, emerged photons a hair inside it, and
the spherical pusher's cone intersection then placed them exactly on $\theta = \pi$,
where the direction is undefined.  On the spherical sphere this was 89 of 2000
photons lost to NaN at $a\tau_0 = 10^5$ and photons at the scattering budget at
$10^6$; the four photons the hot Jupiter lost at its pole in the first R3 runs were
the same defect, not the deck's periodic $\theta$.  Now \texttt{DDMCPoleFace} names
such a face and the lattice gives it no rate, no hop, no emergence, no interface, and
a drift toward it stops a hair inside; the pusher carries a transport photon across
the axis as before.  The hunt found a pre-existing typo in the reflecting outer
$\theta$ boundary of the photon exchange (\texttt{ReflectMCOuterX2} read the radial
faces), fixed in passing.

\textbf{The scattering column's overflow.}  The spherical sphere at $a\tau_0 =
10^6$ then failed with its spectrum far too wide (median $|x|$ 103 against the exact
90, a Kolmogorov distance ten times the sampling scale) and $9.5\times10^6$ analog
scatterings per photon where the Cartesian sphere at the same depth spends five
thousand, nearly all of them at line centre inside diffusion cells, where a transport
photon converts at its first scattering.  An instrumented build showed those photons
carrying a negative scattering count.  A dwell adds the scatterings it stands for to
the photon's integer column \texttt{nscp}, saturating at \texttt{INT\_MAX} when the
sum exceeds it (R1), and the next analog scattering incremented the saturated column
past the limit to a negative number, after which the conversion test's ``has
scattered at least once'' (\texttt{nscp > 0}, which lets a photon just emitted fly
once first) failed for the rest of that photon's life: it walked the whole sphere as
an analog photon.  The spherical mesh resolves the radius with 32 cells where the
Cartesian cube resolves the diameter, so its walkers take four times the dwells and
were the first to reach the limit; a Cartesian run at a greater depth or a finer mesh
would have met it too.  The two analog increments of the column (the scattering
loop and the random walk's step) now saturate as the dwell does.  The column is a
count for the report and the problem generators' user columns, and a saturated value
there means ``more than the column holds''.

\textbf{The leakage rates of a curved cell.}  With the overflow fixed the spherical
sphere at $a\tau_0 = 10^6$ cost what a lattice run costs (1562 scatterings and 1453
dwells per photon) and was still 15 percent too wide in the median, where the
Cartesian sphere at the same depth is right.  The leakage rate of D1 is the box form,
$1/\Delta$ times $D/\delta$ with $D$ the harmonic mean of the diffusion coefficients
on the two sides and $\delta$ the width between the centres, and its $1/\Delta$ is the
face's area over the cell's volume only for a box.  A spherical polar cell has an
outer radial face larger than its inner one, so a walker in the inner cells, where
the shell source puts every photon, leaked inward as readily as outward and stayed
longer than the diffusion equation says; the lattice's time, and with it the
frequency diffusion, ran long, and the spectrum came out wide, by 4 percent at
$a\tau_0 = 10^5$ and 15 at $10^6$ as the lattice's share grew.  The rates now carry
the factor $(A_f/V)\,\Delta$ of each face (\texttt{DDMCFaceFactors}, 1 for a box; for
the innermost cell of the test mesh 0.14 inward and 2.3 outward, 0.97 and 1.03 at the
outer edge, 0 for a face on the axis and up to 2 for the other $\theta$ face of a cell
at the pole, whose cone has more surface away from the axis than the box estimate),
in the grey table and in the per-dwell rates alike, while the frequency rates, which
are per unit path, take none.

node at a wide \key{dx} misstates the core residence the same way (at 10\,000 K and
\key{dx} 21 it is $5.6\times10^6$ scatterings against the kinetic $6.4\times10^4$),
which the spectrum of a static run does not feel and the paths and times do; a run
whose transit time matters wants \key{dx} of order one next to the core, and a
graded bin width is future work.  The core bin forgets the side it was entered from,
where the analog photon keeps a memory of its sign over a core visit, a candidate
cause of the 0.01 to 0.02 red-fraction excess at one Doppler width in R2.  A ghost
across a periodic mesh boundary is not a lattice cell, so a walker emerges and
reconverts at every periodic crossing, a cost and a small interface bias that the
tests carry and R1 left in place.  And the one-dimensional toy's kinetic core carries
no path while its lattice core bin does, so its path comparison overstates the
lattice's path by the core's share, an accounting artefact and not a residence
defect.

\section{What R4 built: the core edge inside the Doppler core}
\label{sec:r4}

\textbf{Why.}  On the production hot Jupiter deck (stellar Lyman alpha and
photoionization, equal weights, the analytic 7000\,K atmosphere of
\texttt{\~{}/mc\_test/hot\_jupiter/combo}; 128 radial cells with \key{x1rat} 1.02 over
1 to 8 planet radii, 33 cells in each angle) the lattice of R3 holds no cell: the base is
$7\times10^4$ deep at line centre across a cell and 1.3 at $|x| = 3.3$.  Yet with 1000
stellar photons per cycle 84 percent of the scatterings fall in cells more than 4000 mean
free paths across at the photon's frequency, 96 percent of the photons never scatter,
and one photon of the thousand made 330\,000 scatterings and lived 157 of the cycle's
157 wall seconds (on one rank the same deck takes 708 s for a different photon of
$10^5$).  The deep photon sits in the Doppler core of a cell that is thick at $|x|
\lesssim 2.7$ and thin outside, and the single global core bin cannot hold it.

\textbf{The per-cell core edge.}  Each cell gets its own core edge $x_c$
(\texttt{ddmc\_line\_xc}, reference Doppler units), the largest $|x|$ at which
$k(x)\,W \ge$ \key{tau} by bisection on the Voigt extinction, capped at \key{core\_width};
a cell whose edge in its own Doppler units is below \key{core\_min} (default 2, where a
kinetic visit is 260 scatterings) is not a diffusion cell.  The wing bins are unchanged
and are nodes only where the full core is (the extinction falls outward, so a partial
core has no wing nodes).  \texttt{DDMCLineNode(cell, e)} tests the energy against the
cell's band in the core bin; \texttt{DDMCLineNodeBin} keeps the bin-level meaning.  An
entry into the node from a scattering is admitted with probability 1: by Kac's lemma
the mean sojourn from the stationary entrance distribution is $1/p_{\rm out}$, so the
stationary exit rate balances the kinetic entries exactly.

\textbf{The visit table.}  The core walker's extinction for the scatterings a dwell
stands for is the mean of $k$ over the node (flat $J$ within a thick core); its exit
through either frequency face is the kinetic rate $k/(2N)$ with $N(x_{\rm loc}, s)$ from
a two-dimensional table over the node's local half-width $x_{\rm loc} = x_c s$ (steps of
0.25 from \key{core\_min} to \key{core\_width} times the largest $s$) and the Doppler
scale $s$ (twelve points in $\log_{10} s$ from $-0.7$ to 0.4), bilinear in $\log N$; the
R3 table was the row $x_{\rm loc} = 3.3\,s$.  A point is one chain of
\texttt{LineCoreVisit}: a photon scattering forever in frequency, a visit opening at a
scattering into $|x| < x_c$ and closing at the first scattering out, so that the entrance
distribution is the stationary one; the chain runs to 200 visits and $10^6$ scatterings,
or 20\,000 visits, and the first (truncated) visit is discarded.  The emergence frequency
is the cell's edge plus an overshoot drawn from the quantiles of the chain's exit
overshoots (65 levels $u = 1 - (1 - t)^2$, denser toward the tail, interpolated
bilinearly in $(x_{\rm loc}, \log s)$).  The toys (\texttt{core\_edge\_toy.py},
\texttt{chain\_toy.py} in \texttt{tst/montecarlo/sphere\_lya}) measured the exit law:
the mean overshoot is 0.19 to 0.23 Doppler widths for $x_c$ of 1.5 to 2.5, close to
exponential ($d_{99} \approx 0.9$), and 0.31 to 0.46 with a heavier tail at 3.0 to 3.3
where the Lorentzian exits join.  The R3 rule, a uniform fraction of a Rayleigh step
(mean 0.63), put the photon three times too far out, into a cell $e^{-2 x_c d}$ thinner.
With the exits sampled, the frequency-only hybrid walk reaches $|x| \ge x_c + 1$ and
$x_c + 2$ in 0.86 to 1.19 times the kinetic walk's visits for $x_c$ of 1.5 to 2.5, with
no trend (the scatter is the tail-dominated statistics of 1000 walkers, and the entry
and the exit of a short visit are correlated, which an independent exit cannot carry);
an exponential of the measured mean ranges from 0.56 to 1.21, and the Rayleigh rule
gives 0.05.  The reach counts are set by the tail of the exit law, so the table samples
a short visit many times.  Two definitions of the visit, the chain and R3's entry from a wing photon
uniform within one Doppler width above the edge, agree in $N$ and in the exit law to a
percent with 20\,000 visits each (a 15 percent disagreement at 2000 visits was the
toy's chain starting inside the core).

The table costs minutes (330 to 390 s at 7000 and 10\,000\,K, every rank building the
same table) against 40 s for the R3 row, so it is written to \key{visit\_file} after
its build and read back when the header (line temperature, core width and minimum,
visit cap, quantile count, grid step and grids) matches; the test driver shares one
file per temperature across the runs of a work directory.

\textbf{The core node's spatial leakage.}  The R3 code diffused the core walker with
the bin-mean $k$, which is $e^{x_c^2}/(2 x_c^2)$ too large at the edge frequencies that
carry the flux (a factor 20 at $x_c = 2.6$, 700 at 3.3); the slab and sphere tests
cannot see it, their core mean free path being $10^{-6}$ of the medium.  With $J$ flat
across the node each face's rate is now the average over the node of the per-frequency
rate $2/(3W(k(x)W + k_n(x)W_n))$, the neighbour's node or the boundary form by frequency
(\texttt{DDMCLineCoreRates}, Simpson on 64 intervals in $x = x_c\cos\theta$, which
resolves the $e^{x^2}$ rise at the edge; the per-dwell rates replace the table's for a
core walker in \texttt{DDMCRates}); the frequency at which the walker crosses a face is
drawn in proportion to that face's per-frequency rate and then tested against the
neighbour's node, hop or emerge at that frequency (\texttt{DDMCLineCoreDraw}; an
advective crossing draws uniformly over the node); the force deposit of a crossing uses
$k$ at that frequency (\texttt{DDMCDepositExtinction}, \texttt{DDMCLineKPhoton}), and so
does the conversion probability of a transport photon at a face.

\textbf{Tests.}  The slab and sphere checks against Neufeld and Dijkstra are unchanged
(medians within 0.13 bin, $K$ 0.014 to 0.035 at $a\tau_0$ of $10^5$ to $10^7$).  A new
check, \texttt{coreedge}: the slab at $\tau_0 = 10^4$ and $10^5$ at $10^4$\,K ($a\tau_0$
4.7 and 47), where no cell is thick at the core bin's edge and every diffusion cell's
core node ends inside the Doppler core (mean edges 2.3 to 3.1), the lattice at 8 and 32
cells against one analog run of 1000 photons: the escaping $x$ within 0.7 to 1.3 of the
two-sample scale, the paths within 1.0 to 1.4 (mean path ratios 0.93 to 1.11), at 14 to
50 analog scatterings per photon against $4.9\times10^4$ and $2.5\times10^5$, 20 to 400
times cheaper per photon.

\textbf{The hot Jupiter.}  The production deck's Lyman alpha type (1000 and 20\,000
stellar photons, equal weights, the ionizing type off so that the generator's
post-transfer hook is untouched) against its analog run with the same seed: 33\,627
diffusion cells, a quarter of the mesh, every one with its core edge inside the core
bin (mean edge 2.18 reference widths); the escaping velocity distribution identical
($K$ 0.001 against a two-sample scale of 0.010 at 20\,000 photons; the bands 0 to 10, 10
to 30, 30 to 60, 60 to 120 km/s hold 2349/2356, 4514/4510, 5642/5625, 5953/5960
photons), the escaped weight equal to $3\times10^{-4}$, the absorbed count 89 against 96.
The lattice's 2874 dwells stand for $1.4\times10^7$ scatterings against the analog run's
$9.4\times10^6$ (the count is dominated by a handful of photons and differs by the
one-photon noise); the longest-lived photon takes 6 s instead of 345, and the cycle's
transport wall time is 6.4 s against 377 (the analog run's 1000-photon cycle, 157 s,
becomes 4.3 s).  The ionizing type, which never scatters, is 0.2 s either way.  The
analog deck's cost is itself remarkable: 1.2 ms per scattering on one rank against
2.6\,$\mu$s on the slab test; a profile is in hand to find out why.

\textbf{What is still approximate.}  The exit law is drawn independently of the entry,
so a short visit's correlation between the two (5 to 10 percent in the visits to reach a
frequency one Doppler width out at $x_c = 1.5$) is lost; \key{core\_min} 2 keeps the
lattice away from the worst of it.  The node's flat-$J$ assumption is marginal in a cell
that is only \key{tau} deep at the edge frequencies, where $J$ is depressed by the escape
at those frequencies, which is the same marginality the spatial lattice carries at
\key{tau} 3.  A cell whose core node would reach beyond the global core bin (a cold cell,
$s > 1$) still ends at \key{core\_width}; the rows of the table above $x_{\rm loc}$ of 5
exist for those cells and cost most of its build.

\textbf{The force, measured.}  Nothing before this measured the lattice's radiation
force.  The hot Jupiter cannot: its lattice and analog runs agree in every lattice cell
(pulls of mean 0.00 and rms 0.67 over 11\,524 cells) but the force there is noise in
both, the net flux through the deep atmosphere being the 90 photons in 20\,000 that
reach the inner boundary.  The slab of \texttt{coreedge} can, every photon crossing the
lattice, with \texttt{mcsrc} and \texttt{mclab} outputs.  Two findings.  The test's
mesh puts the source on a cell face, and the lattice then moves it half a cell: a
converting photon is placed at its cell's centre, so the source sits at the centre of
the cell holding the origin, the escape split goes 0.516/0.484 and the source cell's
flux is zero; a mesh shifted by half a cell removes all of it.  On that mesh the
lattice's flux was 8 percent low beside the source and 1.5 percent low across the slab
against an analog flux flat to 0.1 percent: the move from the scattering point to the
centre at a birth conversion was never deposited, and a photon that enters a diffusion
cell through a face and converts loses the displacement from that side.
\texttt{DDMCConvert} now deposits the move in the flux, which is then conservative to
0.1 percent, and not in the force: charged at a hop's extinction, the node's harmonic
mean, the move made the force 1.6 times the analog one, because outside the core the
slab's flux and force are carried by wing photons at a far smaller extinction and the
converting photon arrived as one of them, its force on that leg in its flight's
deposit.  With that, at 400\,000 lattice and 40\,000 analog photons on the shifted
slab at $\tau_0 = 10^6$: the half-slab force integrals 0.99 and 0.95 of the analog's
with the analog's 7 percent error, the 32 per-cell pulls of mean 0.15 and rms 1.09
(\texttt{RadforceF\_err} is right on the lattice), the lattice's force equal to
$-\partial_x E/3$ of its own energy density cell by cell, the outermost cell 2.6 sigma
high (the boundary cell, as in the cube), the energy density 1.00 to 1.07 of the
analog's across the slab and 1.12 in the edge cells.  The lattice's force in a core
cell is therefore the diffusion closure's, right in the mean to the lattice's order and
with a correct error estimate, and not the noisier estimator the earlier sections
feared: the slab's per-cell errors are a quarter of the analog's at the same photon
count, the dwell replacing thousands of cancelling kicks by a few hops of definite
sign.

\section{Decisions taken before D1}
\label{sec:decisions}

Settled with the user on 2026-10-28.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The emergence distribution: both \key{asymptotic} and \key{isotropic} are built
  behind the key, and the disk atmosphere against Feautrier in D1 picks the default
  (Section~\ref{sec:interface}).  No choice is made before the test.
\item Level jumps: a face whose neighbour block is at another refinement level is a
  boundary face in both directions (a walker emerges through it, a transport
  photon converts on the far side by the usual probability).  Exact, and only costs a
  conversion per crossing; the split-face rates of Densmore et al.\ (2007) are the
  follow-up if the report shows many hops stopped there on the XRB
  (Section~\ref{sec:faces}).
\item The dynamic budget: a held status that the round accounting treats as done and
  the next cycle resumes is wanted, for transport photons as much as for walkers,
  so it is a feature of its own outside the lattice's hook list and not a lattice
  phase.  Until it exists the walker's budget stop copies the photon's
  (Section~\ref{sec:time}), and the Compton phases run static.
\item The threshold is the extinction times the cell's smallest width, one key
  \key{tau} (Section~\ref{sec:rates}).  A cell thick in two directions and thin in the
  third is not a diffusion cell, since the diffusion limit fails along the thin one.
\item Periodic faces are boundary faces in the first build; the report counts the
  emergences through them (Section~\ref{sec:faces}).
\item The lattice runs only with \key{<ddmc> enable = true}, and the \texttt{ddmcp}
  column is registered on the \texttt{Photon} class only then, so a run with the
  feature off has the same photon layout, memory and output bytes as before the
  feature existed.
\end{enumerate}
